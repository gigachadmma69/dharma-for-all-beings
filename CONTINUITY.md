# Carry this forward

This project exists to preserve accurately attributed Buddhist teachings and make them accessible. It does not promise awakening, claim to represent every tradition, or speak as its founder after their death.

## A successor can begin here

1. Download a preservation release and its SHA256SUMS file. Verify the checksum, unzip it, and open dist/index.html. Reading requires neither an account nor a running server.
2. Keep the release on two independent storage providers and an offline drive. A fork under a different custodian's account reduces dependence on the founder's account. These additional custodians and copies have not yet been arranged.
3. To restore the website, upload dist/ to any static host. To change it, edit archive.json and run python3 scripts/build.py. Keep exact wording, source links, translators, context and rights notices intact.
4. To continue social publishing, obtain legitimately delegated account access. Do not copy sessions or credentials from this repository: it contains none. Native X scheduling and the founder's local Codex task are the present mechanism, not an independent hosted publisher.
5. Before automating external publishing, establish a maintained host, persistent deduplication ledger, authorized API access and an explicit operating budget. Verify current service terms and costs. Have a second custodian receive failures. Stop uncertain sends for reconciliation rather than posting duplicates.
6. Every month, restore a release in a clean directory, verify its checksum, read the site offline, check representative citations, and test the actual publishing queue. Annually review custodians, funding, access and host portability.

## Sustainable reuse

Prefer new, verified selections. An old quotation may return only after at least 365 days, after its source and reuse basis have been checked again. Keep each publication as a separate event; never erase publication history. Avoid repeats already in the pending queue and enforce author/work spacing. Do not cycle a tiny library at high frequency. If eligible material runs out, report the gap; do not invent content or silently shorten the interval.

At 96 posts daily, a full year without repeats needs 35,040 eligible quotations. As of 28 September 2026, the repository contains 61 distinct verified excerpts: 43 in the text reading sequence, 2 linked to recordings, and 16 in the additional reserve. These are archive counts, not remaining scheduled posts. A 365-day interval is a policy, not proof that a year of material or autonomous operation exists.

## What is established / what remains

Established: public source repository, a hosted static library, a portable release, a local editorial ledger and recurring local replenishment task.

Not established: independent custodians, separate-account/offline backups, a funded independent AI/X publisher, perpetual hosting or irrevocable institutional preservation. The GitHub research workflow remains an inactive template pending appropriate workflow authorization.

Software is MIT-licensed. Modern translations retain separate rights; the software license cannot authorize unlimited quotation reuse. Prefer permissioned and public-domain translations for long-term expansion, while preserving the founder's research priorities for Padmasambhava, Yeshe Tsogyal and Erik Pema Kunsang.

No calendar date, automation or archive format can guarantee perpetuity. The continuity mechanism is people who can preserve, verify, migrate and take responsibility for it.


## Custodian handoff — not yet assigned

Record the primary caretaker and an independent backup, their explicit acceptance, where independently held copies live, and the date they successfully restored one. Keep private contact information and access arrangements outside this public repository. A name in a document is not delegated access. Never infer consent or death from silence.

A successor should first preserve the library, then decide which distribution channels they can responsibly maintain. The text, source records and feed can move to any static host without an AI service. Automated research and social posting require separately maintained services; their failure must not erase the library.

## Recovery by failure type

| Failure | Immediate action | Resume only after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local computer unavailable | Existing native scheduled posts may continue; retrieve the public release or repository | A caretaker restores the library and verifies the actual X queue |
| Website host unavailable | Read dist/index.html offline or deploy dist to another authorized host | Source links, attributions and feed are checked |
| Repository account unavailable | Use an independently held release; do not assume another copy exists | A caretaker verifies the checksum and establishes a new maintained source home |
| X access lost | Stop X writes; continue library and feed | Legitimate account access returns, or another independently authorized channel is established |
| Quotation disputed | Quarantine that passage from new selection and inspect the cited edition in context | Wording, actual speaker, translator and reuse basis are resolved |
| Uncertain send | Preserve the attempt; inspect published and scheduled posts | The attempt is reconciled; never retry blindly |
| Verified supply insufficient | Report the uncovered slots; preserve the archive | New suitable material is independently reviewed; never invent or shorten the reuse interval silently |

Disruptions should leave a recorded lesson: evidence, cause where known, repair, and a prevention check. Redundancy reduces fragility; learning from real failures is the part that can make the project improve under stress.
